Zendesk's own published entry point, Support Team at $19 per agent, sounds competitive, but it ships with no AI agents at all. To get AI into a Zendesk plan you need Suite Team or higher, and to get past a five-to-fifteen-resolution monthly cap per agent, most small teams end up on Suite Growth with the Advanced AI add-on, which is where the $695 figure for a 5-person team comes from in independent 2026 pricing reviews. Past the included allowance, every additional resolved conversation is billed on top, with no ceiling since Zendesk removed its billing grace period in January 2026.

LiveAgent's Medium plan includes AI Agents with full setup at no extra license fee. The only variable cost is FlowHunt AI usage, billed through a credit system where each account starts with a substantial free allowance and further usage is pay-as-you-go, not per-resolution.

## Why Zendesk's AI gets expensive for small teams specifically

Three things compound for a small business on Zendesk in 2026:

- **Seats climb fast.** Suite Team ($55), Suite Growth ($79 to $89), and Suite Professional ($115) each unlock AI capability tiers, so small teams often need to buy up a plan level just to get usable AI, not just more seats.
- **AI is metered, not flat.** Zendesk bills AI agents per automated resolution, roughly $1.50 to $2.00 each, after a small included allowance. A support team with predictable ticket volume can budget for this. A small business with seasonal spikes, a product launch, or a bad week cannot, because the AI bill moves with volume in a way seat pricing never does.
- **Add-ons stack.** Copilot and Advanced AI typically run about $50 per agent per month on top of the base plan, and quality assurance or workforce management modules add further line items. What looks like a $55 plan on the pricing page often lands closer to $140 per agent once AI is actually usable.

## How LiveAgent's AI pricing works

LiveAgent's AI Answer Assistant and AI chatbot both run on FlowHunt, Quality Unit's own automation platform, which is why the pricing model looks different from a bolt-on AI vendor:

- **No separate AI license fee.** AI Agents are included in LiveAgent's paid plans, with full setup done by LiveAgent's team at no extra cost.
- **Credit-based usage, not per-resolution billing.** FlowHunt uses credits instead of charging per resolved conversation. Each account starts with a free credit allowance, and additional usage is pay-as-you-go, so cost tracks actual AI workload rather than an outcome definition set by the vendor.
- **One flat seat price covers the help desk.** Ticketing, live chat, knowledge base, and AI sit in the same $15 to $69 per agent range, so there's no separate "AI-ready" tier to buy into.

### What a FlowHunt credit actually buys

"Pay-as-you-go" is a vague promise until you see the unit economics. FlowHunt prices usage in credits, where one credit is worth roughly $1 and covers up to 100 flow interactions, though the exact number depends on how complex each interaction is. A simple exchange, like answering "hello" or a one-line FAQ, costs a small fraction of a cent. A longer, multi-step interaction that pulls from a knowledge base or calls an external tool costs more, but still typically a fraction of a credit. In practice, this means:

- Every LiveAgent account starts with a substantial free credit allowance before any billing kicks in.
- Simple, high-volume questions (order status, business hours, password resets) cost the least per interaction, which is exactly the ticket volume small businesses want automated first.
- You can see your AI credits spend broken down by task type rather than receiving a single opaque "AI usage" line item, which makes it easier to spot where automation is paying off and where it isn't.

This is the practical difference between credit-based and per-resolution pricing: a credit is spent on the interaction itself, not on a vendor's definition of what counts as a "resolution."

## How to estimate your own AI cost before switching

Rather than taking either vendor's marketing at face value, a small business can estimate its likely AI spend in about four steps:

1. **Pull your ticket or chat volume for a typical month.** Most help desks show this in reporting; aim for total conversations, not just tickets closed by an agent.
2. **Estimate what share is repetitive.** FAQs, order status, account questions, and password resets are usually the first candidates for automation, commonly 40 to 75% of total volume depending on the business.
3. **Apply each vendor's unit cost to that share.** For Zendesk, multiply the automated share by $1.50 to $2.00 per resolution once you're past the included allowance. For LiveAgent, estimate credits based on interaction complexity, most simple support interactions cost a small fraction of a credit.
4. **Add the seat cost for your actual team size.** This is where flat-fee plans and per-resolution plans diverge most, since seat cost is fixed but resolution cost scales with the volume you just estimated in step 2.

Running this exercise on your own numbers, rather than a generic team-size table, is the only way to know which pricing model actually favors your specific ticket mix.

## Hidden costs to check on either platform

No pricing comparison is complete without naming where each vendor's list price doesn't tell the whole story.

**On Zendesk:** the seat price is the floor, not the ceiling. Beyond the Advanced AI add-on, Copilot, quality assurance, and workforce management modules are each separate line items, commonly $50 or more per agent per month apiece. Multi-brand support and enhanced data privacy sit behind higher tiers as well.

**On LiveAgent:** the base plan covers ticketing, live chat, knowledge base, and AI, but social media channel integrations (Facebook, Instagram, and similar) carry an additional cost on the Small and Medium plans, which can push teams that want full omnichannel coverage toward the Large plan. WhatsApp support runs through a third-party provider (Twilio or 360dialog) rather than a native integration.

**On both:** monthly billing runs meaningfully higher than annual, typically 20 to 30% more, so list prices quoted annually understate the cost for a team not ready to commit to a yearly contract.
